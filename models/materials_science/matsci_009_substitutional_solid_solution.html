Substitutional Solid Solution

Materials Science

How It Works

A substitutional solid solution forms when solute atoms directly substitute for host solvent atoms on regular crystal lattice sites. Under the Hume-Rothery rules, extensive solid solubility requires that solute and solvent atomic radii differ by less than 15%, have identical crystal structures, and possess similar electronegativities and valencies to minimize internal lattice strain.

Governing Equation
$$ \Delta r = \frac{|r_{\text{solute}} - r_{\text{solvent}}|}{r_{\text{solvent}}} \le 15\% $$
