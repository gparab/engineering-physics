How It Works

Double-stranded DNA functions mechanically as a semiflexible polymer with high torsional stiffness. In topologically constrained genomic domains, the fundamental topological linking number (Lk) is partitioned between double-helical twist (Tw) and tertiary superhelical writhe (Wr). Torsional stress generated during transcription and replication induces plectonemic supercoils that regulate gene expression.

Governing Equation
$$ \text{Lk} = \text{Tw} + \text{Wr} \quad\vert\quad \Delta\text{Lk} = \Delta\text{Tw} + \Delta\text{Wr} \quad\vert\quad E_{\text{torsion}} = \frac{1}{2} C \left(\frac{2\pi \Delta\text{Tw}}{L}\right)^2 $$
