VLSI CMOS Inverter

Electronics Engineering

How It Works

The complementary metal-oxide-semiconductor (CMOS) inverter is the fundamental building block of modern VLSI digital microprocessor logic, consisting of a pMOS pull-up transistor and an nMOS pull-down transistor fabricated upon a single silicon die. With a shared polysilicon gate input, exactly one transistor conducts at any logic state, driving the output cleanly to V_DD or ground with rail-to-rail voltage swing and zero static DC power dissipation.

Governing Equation
$$ V_{\text{out}} = V_{\text{DD}} - V_{\text{in}} \quad\vert\quad P_{\text{dyn}} = \alpha C_L V_{\text{DD}}^2 f_{\text{clk}} \quad\vert\quad P_{\text{stat}} \approx I_{\text{leak}} V_{\text{DD}} $$
