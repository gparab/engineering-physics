Geothermal Temperature Conduction Gradient

Geophysical Engineering

How It Works

Earth's geothermal temperature gradient is governed by Fourier's law of thermal conduction coupled with radiogenic heat production in crustal rocks (uranium, thorium, and potassium). Temperature increases with depth from the cold surface boundary condition through the upper granitic and lower basaltic crust, reaching near-magmatic temperatures at the Moho boundary.

Governing Equation
$$ q = -k \frac{dT}{dz} \quad\vert\quad \frac{d^2 T}{dz^2} = -\frac{A_{\text{rad}}}{k} \quad\vert\quad T(z) = T_0 + \frac{q_0}{k} z - \frac{A z^2}{2k} $$
