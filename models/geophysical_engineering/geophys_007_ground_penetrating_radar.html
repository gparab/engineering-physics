Ground Penetrating Radar (GPR) Reflection

Geophysical Engineering

How It Works

Ground Penetrating Radar (GPR) uses high-frequency electromagnetic pulses (10–1000 MHz) to detect subsurface boundaries and buried infrastructure. Dielectric permittivity contrasts (relative permittivity ε_r) between geological media reflect EM wave pulses back to the receiver antenna. As the antenna moves along a survey line over a point reflector (such as a buried pipe), reflection travel times map out a classic hyperbolic profile.

Governing Equation
v_em = c / √ε_r , R = (√ε_r1 - √ε_r2) / (√ε_r1 + √ε_r2)
