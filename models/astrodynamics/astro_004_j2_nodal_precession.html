Earth Oblateness J2 Nodal Precession

Astrodynamics

How It Works

Earth's equatorial bulge (characterized by the dominant second zonal harmonic J₂ = 1.0826 × 10⁻³) exerts a gravitational torque that perturbs Keplerian orbits. This torque causes a secular nodal regression dΩ/dt, shifting the line of nodes along the equator. By matching this regression rate to Earth's annual solar motion (≈ 0.9856°/day), Sun-synchronous orbits maintain a constant local solar illumination angle throughout the year.

Governing Equation
$$ \frac{d\Omega}{dt} = -\frac{3}{2} J_2 \left(\frac{R_E}{p}\right)^2 n \cos i \quad\vert\quad i_{\text{sun-sync}} \approx 98.2^\circ $$
