Superfluid Helium-II Thermomechanical Fountain Effect

Cryogenic Engineering

How It Works

Below the lambda point (T_λ = 2.17 K), liquid helium transitions into the superfluid He-II state, characterized by zero viscosity and two-fluid hydrodynamics. In the thermomechanical (fountain) effect, a porous superleak plug allows only the frictionless superfluid component to pass. Heating the chamber interior creates a thermal gradient that drives an intense osmotic superfluid inflow, generating high thermomechanical pressure that erupts into a continuous liquid fountain jet.

Governing Equation
$$ \Delta P = \rho S \Delta T \quad\vert\quad \vec{v}_s = \frac{\hbar}{m} \nabla\theta $$
