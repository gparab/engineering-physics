Cryogenic Thermal Conductivity & Electronic Mean Free Path

Cryogenic Engineering

How It Works

At cryogenic temperatures below 10 K, lattice phonon vibrations freeze out (decaying as T³), causing thermal conduction in pure metals to be overwhelmingly dominated by conduction electrons. The thermal conductivity becomes linearly proportional to temperature and is strictly bounded by the electronic mean free path limited by crystal impurities. High-purity metals with large Residual Resistivity Ratios (RRR) exhibit orders-of-magnitude higher conductivity than alloys like stainless steel.

Governing Equation
$$ \kappa(T) = \frac{1}{3} C_v v_F \ell_e \approx W_0^{-1} T \quad\vert\quad \frac{\kappa}{\sigma T} = L_0 $$
