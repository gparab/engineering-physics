OFDM

Telecommunications Engineering

How It Works

Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing (OFDM) is a multi-carrier digital modulation technique employed extensively in 4G LTE, 5G NR, and modern Wi-Fi networks. It divides a high-speed data stream across hundreds of closely spaced, overlapping subcarriers whose frequency spacing Δf = 1/T_sym satisfies the mathematical orthogonality condition. This orthogonality ensures that at each subcarrier's center frequency, all other subcarriers produce a spectral null, completely eliminating inter-carrier interference (ICI) without spectral guard bands.

Governing Equation
$$ \Delta f = \frac{1}{T_{\text{sym}}} \quad\vert\quad s(t) = \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} X_k e^{j 2\pi k \Delta f t} \quad\vert\quad \langle e^{j 2\pi k \Delta f t}, e^{j 2\pi m \Delta f t} \rangle = \delta_{km} $$
