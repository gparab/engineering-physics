Acoustic Doppler Frequency Shift

Acoustics Engineering

How It Works

The acoustic Doppler effect describes the frequency shift perceived by an observer due to relative motion between the acoustic source and receiver. As the source moves through the stationary fluid, successive spherical wavefronts are emitted from updated spatial origins. Wavefronts crowd together ahead of the source, resulting in a compressed observed wavelength and higher frequency, while wavefronts trailing the source stretch apart into a lower perceived pitch.

Governing Equation
f_obs = f₀ · (c ± v_rec) / (c ∓ v_src) , λ_ahead = λ₀ · (1 - (v_s / c))
