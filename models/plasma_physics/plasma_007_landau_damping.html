Collisionless Landau Damping

Plasma Physics

How It Works

Landau damping is the collisionless decay of longitudinal electrostatic waves in a plasma resulting from wave-particle resonance. Particles traveling slightly slower than the wave phase velocity (v < v_ph) absorb energy from the electric field, while particles traveling slightly faster (v > v_ph) give up energy. For standard Maxwellian distributions where ∂f₀/∂v < 0, there are more slower particles, yielding net energy transfer from wave to particles and exponential damping.

Governing Equation
$$ \gamma_L = \frac{\pi \omega_p^3}{2 k^2}\left.\frac{\partial f_0}{\partial v}\right|_{v = \omega/k} \quad\vert\quad E(t) = E_0 e^{\gamma_L t}\cos(kx - \omega t) $$
